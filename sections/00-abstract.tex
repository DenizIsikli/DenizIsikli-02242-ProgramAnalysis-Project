% Abstract: a short description of the paper (~150-200 words).
Developers often edit code in ways they believe preserve its behaviour: they inline a
temporary, flip a branch, or drop a guard that looks redundant. Tests rarely hit the one
input where such an edit is wrong, and asking an SMT solver whether two whole methods are
equivalent gives a single yes or no, and no answer at all once the methods contain loops.
We present a checker that answers the question per \emph{region} of the input space. It
analyses the original and the edited JVM method independently with an interval abstract
interpreter on a box of inputs. Sound verdict rules turn the two outcome sets into
\pres{}, \chg{} or \unk{} for the whole box. Undecided boxes are split at the constants
of the code, and only regions where both versions provably return normally are passed to
a 32-bit bit-vector SMT query over their return values. Every counterexample found by the solver
is replayed concretely. On 20 method pairs over two input domains, with ground truth from exhaustive
execution, the checker never gives a wrong verdict. It decides 38 of 40 pair/domain
combinations and certifies 96\% of the inputs on average. One SMT query over the whole
domain decides 34 and certifies 45\%, and differential testing decides 18 and certifies
2\%. The interval layer also decides loop pairs on which symbolic execution gives up.
